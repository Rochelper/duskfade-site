Duskfade Review: A Love Letter Worth Reading

Is Weird Beluga's clockpunk action platformer worth your time and money? We've aggregated media impressions, Steam player feedback, and our own hands-on experience to give you the full picture.

Quick Verdict

The Short Answer

Yes, buy it. If you have any nostalgia for PS2-era 3D action platformers (Kingdom Hearts, Jak & Daxter, Ratchet & Clank), Duskfade is the best spiritual successor in years. The demo's 90% positive rating across 329+ Steam reviews tells you everything you need to know — the feel lands for most people.

What Players Love

  • The time-freeze dodge — the signature mechanic that makes combat click
  • Visual quality — "looks like a Pixar movie" is the most common praise
  • Movement feel — fluid, responsive, "Jak & Daxter but modern"
  • Exploration rewards — side paths consistently yield upgrades, not just currency
  • Cuckoo companion — charming, not annoying, with real gameplay function
  • Soundtrack — widely praised as surprisingly high quality for an indie

What Players Criticize

  • Combat simplicity — basic sword combos may feel shallow vs souls-likes
  • Lock-on system — can be finicky when multiple enemies are present
  • Enemy feedback — hit reactions could be more impactful
  • Camera — occasional issues in tight platforming sections
  • Demo length — some wanted more than 1 hour (addressed in June update)

PC Gamers: Check Your Specs

Duskfade requires an RTX 2060 8GB minimum — unusually steep for an indie game. The Unreal Engine 5 visuals demand real GPU power. Check the demo system requirements before buying.

About Weird Beluga Studio

Weird Beluga Studio is a Spanish indie developer based in Madrid. Their debut game, Clid the Snail (2021), won awards through Sony's PlayStation Talents program and was praised for its unique top-down shooter gameplay. Duskfade is their second title and first 3D action platformer.

The narrative was shaped by Ricardo Chorques Mesa, whose real experience of losing his grandfather inspired the game's themes of grief and memory. Each boss represents an emotional hurdle — Wrath, Sorrow, Fear, Jealousy, and Despair — making the combat a metaphor for personal growth.
